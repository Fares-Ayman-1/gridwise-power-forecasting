    How it works

    • Only what is known in time. A forecast for day D uses prices up to D-1 and weather forecasts made 48 hours ahead. The grid operators' own wind and solar forecasts are left out because they are published after the auction closes.
    • Ranges that mean what they say. Models predict 13 quantiles per hour. Conformal calibration then adjusts them every day from recent errors, so a 90% range holds the real price about 90% of the time.
    • From forecast to decision. An optimiser picks the hours to charge and discharge under the battery's power, capacity, efficiency and wear limits.
    • Scored in public. The forecast file for each day is committed before the prices exist. Days forecast late are flagged and never scored.
